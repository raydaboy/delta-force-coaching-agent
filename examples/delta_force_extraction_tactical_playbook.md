# Delta Force Extraction: Tactical Playbook & Session Guide

> **Scope:** This player guide is a sanitized, source-bound reference derived from an approved coaching session. It contains no gameplay media, private paths, screenshots, or unreviewed claims.

## Match Objective

Prioritize extracting high-value loot. Win only forced close-quarters engagements that threaten the route, position, or secured value. Improve movement, positioning, and crosshair placement without turning optional combat into the objective.

## Match Overview

### Key Strengths

| Strength | What the session supports | Why it matters next match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pressure recovery** | The player uses cover breaks and health recovery before re-engaging from a fresh sightline. | This prevents feeding the same predictable angle after taking damage. |
| **Value density** | Direct gear upgrades and high-tier equipment improve inventory efficiency. | Better items per slot protect carrying capacity and total extraction value. |
| **Endgame execution** | Gathered inventory value is converted into a successful extraction during a high-pressure route sequence. | The run is only valuable once it is converted into an exit. |

### Primary Adjustments

| Priority | Adjustment | Next-match rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Exposure response** | Take visible cover immediately when a scan or exposure warning appears. | Do not sprint down the exposed lane before deciding whether to duel or rotate. |
| **2. Post-fight security** | Clear the primary firing lane and nearest entry point before opening a container or full-screen menu. | A won fight does not automatically make the lane safe to loot. |
| **3. Exit lock** | Pivot directly to extraction after a major upgrade, full backpack, or target value spike. | Avoid a secondary loot loop that turns secured value into unnecessary exposure. |

## Tactical Decision Breakdown

### Scenario 1: Exposure Warning Response

**Trigger:** `Position Exposed` warning or scan alert.

**What happened:** **OBSERVED:** a detection warning appears before the player continues down an open lane. **INFERRED:** continued direct travel increases the risk of taking the first exchange without a reset route. **UNKNOWN:** the exact scan source, enemy count, and every available cover route are not established by the visible moment.

**Corrective action:** Immediately break line of sight using a nearby visible hard-cover edge, stop sprinting, and identify the primary firing lane before shooting or rotating. This costs a few seconds of tempo, but it can preserve the first decision instead of forcing an open reaction duel.

**Execution rule:** `Scan trigger → seek cover → identify lane → engage or rotate`

### Scenario 2: Interior Re-engagements

**Trigger:** Taking heavy damage during a close-quarters engagement.

**What happened:** **OBSERVED:** the player takes damage, uses recovery tools, and later re-engages from a changed line. **INFERRED:** re-peeking the same door frame immediately would make the player easier to pre-aim. **UNKNOWN:** not every interior route or hostile position is visible.

**Corrective action:** Fall back behind the visible break, complete health or limb repairs, then retake from a different height, angle, or entry point. The trade-off is immediate finishing tempo; the likely benefit is a less predictable re-entry rather than a guaranteed win.

**Execution rule:** `Break sightline → repair → change angle → retake`

### Scenario 3: Post-Fight Security

**Trigger:** Winning a firefight or locating a loot container on an active walkway.

**What happened:** **OBSERVED:** a loot opportunity appears after contact near an active lane. **INFERRED:** opening the screen immediately removes information and reaction time if a follow-up threat uses the same approach. **UNKNOWN:** a follow-up hostile is not automatically present just because the lane is active.

**Corrective action:** Return to a nearby stable edge, hold the main entry lane for three seconds, then open the container. The trade-off is delayed looting; the likely benefit is avoiding an information-locked loot screen on a recently contested route.

**Execution rule:** `Eliminate threat → hold lane (3s) → access loot`

### Scenario 4: Full-Screen Interface Safety

**Trigger:** Accessing long decode tasks, map screens, or extensive inventory management.

**What happened:** **OBSERVED:** full-screen interfaces remove much of the immediate visual scene. **INFERRED:** a quick doorway or cover-edge check before opening the interface preserves more useful information. **UNKNOWN:** the source does not prove that a nearby enemy is present during every long interface interaction.

**Corrective action:** Position near a doorway or cover edge, pause for a short audio and lane check, then commit to the interface. This slows the interaction slightly, but it can reduce the risk of starting a long task without current route information.

**Execution rule:** `Edge check → audio scan → open interface`

### Scenario 5: Extraction Transition

**Trigger:** Securing a major loot upgrade, full backpack, or high run value.

**What happened:** **OBSERVED:** late-session value, route, and extraction progress are visible. **INFERRED:** later contacts serve the exit route more than an optional fight chase. **UNKNOWN:** the recording does not prove why every possible exit or detour was accepted or rejected.

**Corrective action:** Pivot the objective to extraction. Fully heal, reload, identify the exit route, and protect that route without nonessential detours. The trade-off is leaving possible loot behind; the likely benefit is protecting the accumulated run value before extraction.

**Execution rule:** `Value secured → status reset → direct extract`

## Core Rules & Practice Plan

### Core Operating Rules

| Trigger | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Warning** | `Warning → cover first.` Use a line-of-sight break before deciding whether to duel or rotate. |
| **Kill / loot opportunity** | `Kill → 3-second check.` Scan the primary firing lane and entry points before opening any loot screen. |
| **Value spike** | `Value spike → route lock.` Reset status, commit to the exit, and eliminate secondary looting loops. |

### Execution Drills

| Drill | Trigger and action | Measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Warning-to-cover drill** | On the next three detection warnings, force a line-of-sight break before firing a shot. | Track whether direct travel stopped before the first shot in all three contacts. |
| **Security-check drill** | On the next three post-fight loot opportunities, hold the main entry lane for three seconds before opening the loot screen. | Complete the check three consecutive times. |
| **Exit-lock drill** | Once target run value is reached, call out the exit route and extract without optional detours. | Complete one extraction-focused run without restarting the loot loop. |

## Evidence Limits

This playbook is based on the approved coaching evidence for the session. It does not establish hidden enemy intent, unseen teammates, unshown geometry, exact scan source, or a guaranteed result from any alternative. Treat the execution rules as repeatable ways to improve information, positioning, and goal protection—not promises of a specific outcome.
