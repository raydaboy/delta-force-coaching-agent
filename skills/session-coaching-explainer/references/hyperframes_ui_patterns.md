# HyperFrames Coaching UI Patterns

## Evidence chip

Use small semantic labels: `OBSERVED` for directly visible or audible facts, `INFERRED` for tactical interpretation, and `UNKNOWN` for unavailable information. Keep the label near the claim it qualifies.

## Causal timeline

Use four or five steps maximum. Example:

`SEES MOVEMENT → THROWS FRAG → ROUTE APPEARS → RESOURCE UNAVAILABLE`

Animate a progress line or marker to show sequence, not certainty. The timeline should reflect source events, not a hypothetical replay.

## Tactical board

Use a dark background and simple CSS geometry for cover, routes, sightlines, and resource zones. State that it is a simplified geometry model. If a threat is off-screen, show the open angle or exposure window without drawing an enemy.

## Question card

Use one question, one frozen decision frame, and a visible thinking window. The question must be answerable from the evidence shown. Do not reveal the answer in the same visual layer as the question.

## Alternative comparison

Use two columns: `ORIGINAL ACTION` and `LIKELY BETTER OPTION`. Include the trade-off under both. Use `likely`, `may`, or `would preserve` language; do not use guaranteed outcomes.

## Scorecard

Separate strengths from priorities. Keep the scorecard readable and limited to the top three corrections. Include confidence or evidence notes when the session is incomplete or ambiguous.

## Motion restraint

Use motion to reveal causality: timeline progress, route drawing, evidence-frame fade, rewind, question-to-reveal transition, and alternative highlight. Avoid gratuitous zooms, spinning text, decorative particles, or reaction graphics.
