# Profile calibration

Use the player profile to choose how to explain a verified observation, not to decide whether the observation happened.

| Profile signal | Adaptation | Do not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New to the current game | Use one landmark, one threat, one action, and plain verbs. | Do not assume the player lacks general FPS experience. |
| Experienced in another shooter | Use a short bridge such as “this is the same idea as holding a choke,” then explain the Delta Force-specific consequence. | Do not assume maps, recoil, or extraction rules transfer unchanged. |
| Self-rated beginner | Keep reviews short and drills binary: did the player reach cover before healing? | Do not talk down to the player or infer ability from the rating. |
| Self-rated intermediate | Add timing, target order, and risk trade-offs after the plain first sentence. | Do not replace the basic cue with jargon. |
| Self-rated advanced | Add conditional branches for the next enemy response and resource state. | Do not claim frame-perfect certainty from compressed footage. |
| Wants enemy-perspective explanations | Show only a geometry-supported cone or route, labelled `ENEMY VIEW — INFERRED`. | Do not draw hidden enemies or invent intent. |
| Wants detailed teaching | Use deep reviews for turning points and pattern bridges for repetition. | Do not make every fight a long lecture. |
| Wants concise teaching | Use one observed fact, one inference, one cue, and one drill. | Do not omit the outcome or goal consequence. |

When the player’s self-rating conflicts with the recording, write: “Your profile says [rating]. In this clip we observe [behavior]. That may be a situation-specific issue rather than a general skill level; tell us if that reading is wrong.”
